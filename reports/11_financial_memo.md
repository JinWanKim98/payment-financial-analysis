**Evidence and interpretation**

- **Growth and reported profitability.** FY2021-2025 revenue rose 65.94% at Visa and 73.64% at Mastercard, on each issuer's own fiscal basis. In FY2025, Visa expenses grew 29.80% while operating income grew 1.69%. Litigation provision rose 2,100, accounting for 57.14% of the 3,675 expense increase; this is accounting attribution, not cash paid or a normalized margin. [1]
- **Operating and net margins differ.** Mastercard operating income rose 21.27%, but net margin moved from 45.71% to 45.65%. The 1,230 increase in reported tax expense partly offsets pretax-income growth; it does not establish a single tax cause or equal cash taxes paid. [2]
- **Cash support needs composition checks.** Both issuers' OCF exceeded consolidated net income in all five reviewed years, yet Visa FY2024 OCF fell 805 while net income rose. FY2025 OCF less defined capital payments gives FCF proxies of 21,577 / 16,433 (Visa/Mastercard). Selected carrying debt increased at both issuers, while cash increased more and debt-minus-cash declined. These observations do not prove recurring cash or maturity coverage. [1,2]


- **Network activity and deductions.** FY2025 purchase volume was USD14,217bn at Visa and USD8,799bn at Mastercard, up 7.76% and 9.95% on the selected nominal fiscal-year series. Processed/switched counts grew 10.18% and 10.06%. Visa client incentives rose 14.44%; derived Mastercard payment-network deductions rose 16.41%. These series preserve different network scopes and do not support a common take-rate. Mastercard FY2023 derived deductions are USD15,181m versus USD15,182m in the narrative, reflecting rounded inputs. See `references/industry_evidence.json` for source URLs, pages and calculations.

**Sensitivity.** Illustration only: Visa revenue growth of 4% and a 56% operating margin yield 23,296 operating income versus 23,994 actual. Growth alone need not increase profit. These chosen assumptions are not guidance, a forecast or an internal budget.
